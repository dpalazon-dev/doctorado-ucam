# Researcher Operational Model

## Purpose

This document describes the operational reality of a researcher.

Its objective is not to define software features, but to understand the environment in which the Research Operating System must operate.

Doctorado_UCAM is designed around the researcher rather than around isolated software capabilities.

Understanding the operational model is therefore a prerequisite for designing the system.

---

# The Researcher's Mission

The primary mission of a researcher is to generate new knowledge.

However, scientific research is only one part of the researcher's daily activity.

Research exists alongside project management, communication, teaching, institutional responsibilities and continuous learning.

The Research Operating System must therefore support the complete operational environment rather than focusing exclusively on research.

---

# Operational Domains

The daily work of a researcher naturally decomposes into several operational domains.

These domains are not isolated.

They constantly exchange information and continuously interrupt one another.

## Research

Activities directly related to scientific discovery.

Examples include:

- Literature review
- Reading papers
- Knowledge acquisition
- Critical analysis
- Hypothesis generation
- Experiment design
- Experiment execution
- Result interpretation
- Scientific writing
- Publication

The purpose of research is to continuously transform information into validated knowledge.

---

## Project Management

Research rarely exists independently.

Most activities belong to projects with defined objectives, collaborators, deadlines and deliverables.

Typical activities include:

- Planning
- Task management
- Progress tracking
- Documentation
- Deliverable preparation
- Meetings
- Decision tracking

Projects provide operational structure for research activities.

---

## Knowledge Management

Knowledge is continuously produced, consumed and refined.

Typical activities include:

- Capturing ideas
- Organizing documents
- Maintaining notes
- Connecting concepts
- Reviewing previous work
- Building personal knowledge

Knowledge management is continuous rather than episodic.

---

## Communication

Research requires constant communication.

Examples include:

- Thesis supervisor
- Project coordinators
- Research collaborators
- Students
- Administrative staff

Communication continuously modifies the operational state of the researcher.

Emails, meetings and conversations frequently introduce new work, new priorities and new knowledge.

---

## Teaching

Academic work frequently includes teaching responsibilities.

These may include:

- Course preparation
- Student support
- Material creation
- Evaluation
- Classroom activities

Teaching should coexist naturally with research rather than becoming an isolated workflow.

---

## Institutional Responsibilities

Doctoral studies introduce additional operational requirements beyond research itself.

Examples include:

- Administrative procedures
- Mandatory courses
- Documentation
- Regulations
- Deadlines
- Institutional platforms

Although they do not directly contribute to scientific discovery, they remain part of the researcher's operational environment.

---

# Operational Characteristics

The researcher's work exhibits several fundamental characteristics.

## Continuous Context Switching

Research is rarely performed without interruption.

Daily work continuously alternates between:

- research
- communication
- project management
- teaching
- administration

Maintaining continuity across interruptions becomes a significant cognitive challenge.

---

## Knowledge-Centric

Nearly every activity depends on existing knowledge.

Knowledge should therefore remain persistent, connected and continuously reusable.

---

## Context-Dependent

The value of information depends heavily on context.

A paper, note or experiment cannot be interpreted independently from the project, hypothesis, collaborators and historical decisions surrounding it.

Context preservation is therefore a core operational requirement.

---

## Non-Linear

Research does not follow a deterministic sequence.

Activities evolve organically.

Ideas emerge unexpectedly.

Projects change priorities.

Experiments fail.

New evidence modifies previous assumptions.

The operational model must therefore support exploration rather than rigid workflows.

---

## Long-Term Continuity

Research projects often span years.

The system must preserve continuity across long periods of inactivity.

Returning to an unfinished idea months later should require minimal cognitive effort.

---

# Operational Friction

Current research workflows suffer from several recurring sources of friction.

Examples include:

- Fragmented software ecosystems
- Repeated context switching
- Duplicate information
- Manual document organization
- Knowledge becoming disconnected over time
- Difficulty recovering previous reasoning
- Administrative overload
- Repetitive operational tasks

These frictions consume cognitive resources without contributing to scientific value.

---

# Desired Operational State

The ideal operational environment enables the researcher to concentrate on reasoning rather than coordination.

The researcher should spend the majority of cognitive effort on:

- understanding
- questioning
- connecting ideas
- designing experiments
- writing
- discovering knowledge

Operational overhead should progressively disappear into the background.

Software should become infrastructure rather than attention.
